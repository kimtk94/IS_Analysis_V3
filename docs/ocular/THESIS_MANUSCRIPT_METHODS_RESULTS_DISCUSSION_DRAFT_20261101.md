## Abstract — manuscript-ready *conservative* working draft

**Background.** Glucocorticoid exposure alters gene expression in trabecular meshwork (TM) cells, but differences in cell annotation, experimental design and processing can complicate transcriptomic inference. We examined the reproducibility of TM glucocorticoid-associated transcriptional responses across publicly accessible experiments and evaluated sensitivity to cell-selection decisions.

**Methods.** We reconstructed raw UMI pseudobulks from a 10x single-cell TM dataset (GSE313736), retaining 57 donor-paired dexamethasone (DEX) and vehicle samples for the primary BeamA analysis. Differential expression was analyzed using donor-blocked edgeR quasi-likelihood models. We repeated the analysis at two BeamA confidence thresholds and with an alternative scPred TM_Fibro annotation using the same 55 overlapping eligible donors. Gene-effect directions were compared with published 100 nM, 48-hour bulk TM results and normalized 500 nM, seven-day bulk TM data (GSE301517), separating three named TM strains from their technical replicates; the three strains' independent-donor provenance was not established.

**Results.** In the original source, 13,717 of 16,050 tested genes met a BH-FDR threshold of 0.05, a very broad response that warrants explicit consideration of batch and selection effects. The 57-donor BeamA confidence reanalysis yielded a gene-level log2 fold-change correlation of 0.999 between thresholds 0.75 and 0.50. Within the fixed 55 donors, the BeamA versus scPred annotation comparison yielded a correlation of 0.950 among 13,975 genes testable in both models. MYOC, TGFBI and MMP2 retained effect direction and statistical significance in both within-source sensitivity analyses. These three transcripts also had the same change direction across three experimental sources, whereas ACTA2 was directionally discordant in the seven-day dataset. Across 194 commonly annotated Hallmark EMT genes, 124 showed concordant aggregate directions in the three settings, whereas only **90 of 194** also agreed in all three seven-day TM strains. The discovery dataset used **18 condition-specific 10x libraries**; a design with unrestricted source-library effects plus treatment had 19 columns but rank 18, so the DEX coefficient was not separately identifiable from arbitrary library effects. The cross-study results are descriptive; the published erratum discloses raw-read availability without reporting corrected gene estimates, and an independent source raw-count refit has not been conducted.

**Conclusions.** These public-data reanalyses support prioritization of selected glucocorticoid-associated TM transcripts and demonstrate numerical robustness to the tested cell-annotation choices, while identifying lower direction stability among weak effects and a structural DEX–source-library confounding boundary. The results do not establish treatment-independent causality, ECM protein/function changes or an individual steroid-induced glaucoma risk prediction.

## 1. Introduction (evidence-proportional draft)

Elevated intraocular pressure (IOP) is a major modifiable risk factor for glaucomatous optic neuropathy, and the trabecular meshwork regulates a substantial fraction of aqueous humor outflow resistance. Corticosteroid exposure can cause ocular hypertension in susceptible individuals, prompting investigation of glucocorticoid-responsive pathways in human TM cells. Previous experimental work has documented MYOC induction, changes in matrix-related transcripts, cytoskeletal reorganization, and—in some systems—senescence-related phenotypes following DEX exposure.

RNA profiling of primary TM cultures has already been reported at different DEX exposure durations, including a 2022 study assessing shorter and seven-day conditions and a subsequent 2026 peer-reviewed study of 100 nM treatment at 1, 6 and 48 hours in TM and Schlemm's canal endothelial cultures. A separate 2026 preprint integrated DEX-responsive TM RNA profiles with chromatin topology and glaucoma-related genetic association signals. These studies make the detection of glucocorticoid-responsive transcript and ECM pathways an established biological observation rather than a novel discovery by itself.

However, computational comparisons of public datasets can be sensitive to source populations, donor/technical replication, cell-state annotation and treatment-linked sequencing batches. In the single-cell study GSE313736, vehicle and DEX cells were processed as condition-specific 10x libraries, and the chosen BeamA cell label was assigned after treatment. We therefore examined which transcript-effect signs are stable across three different TM experiments and whether a selected signal persists in the original dataset when the cell-confidence threshold or the TM annotation system changes. We present direction and methodological robustness as **candidate-prioritization evidence**, not as evidence that transcriptomic signatures cause glaucoma or clinically predict steroid-induced IOP elevation.

**Aim 1:** quantifiably reconstruct donor-level transcript effects, retaining explicit unit-of-analysis and provenance.  
**Aim 2:** evaluate the sensitivity of these effects to BeamA confidence and independent cell-label algorithms with a fixed shared donor set.  
**Aim 3:** describe signed consistency in two other public TM experimental systems without incorrectly pooling non-comparable effect-size estimators.

## 2. Materials and Methods

### 2.1 Public evidence sources, experimental doses and biological units

The discovery resource was GEO GSE313736, a 10x single-cell RNA-seq dataset from cultured human TM cells exposed to vehicle or 500 nM DEX at the sample-level stated 48-hour condition. Its GEO Series description reports 74 source donors and 316,799 singlet cells, whereas the present strictly reconstructed analysis uses 18 original 10x libraries from nine source pools and **57 donor-paired profiles** passing the fixed criteria defined below. The GEO summary and recovered count-matrix populations are not interchangeable denominators.

The second, independent external transcript source was Mehrotra et al., 2026, an *Investigative Ophthalmology & Visual Science* journal article describing primary TM cultures from nine QC-passed strains treated with vehicle or 100 nM DEX for 48 hours, with earlier 1- and 6-hour experiments. We used the published full-gene summary values rather than refitting that study's raw sample count data. **The October 2026 erratum adds a Data Availability paragraph identifying SRA PRJNA1243143; it does not report a correction of gene effects.** The actual ENA source archive has 46 runs (92 FASTQ files, ~89.68GB compressed), with 17 Vehicle/DEX pairs explicitly labeled in the RUN aliases and six additional `-1/-2` library pairs whose matched 12 official BioSample XML treatment attributes confirm six Vehicle and six 100 nM DEX arms (23/23 candidate pairs documented). Before a new raw-count reanalysis, each record's specific 1h/6h/48h timepoint, QC eligibility and underlying independent culture/strain unit must be verified. We have NOT independently re-fit these raw data.

The third, separately conducted study was the 2026 **Singh et al. preprint** underlying GEO GSE301517. Its GEO sample metadata indicated 21 RNA samples, comprising seven Resource samples, seven Control samples and seven DEX samples. Treatment/control profiles were obtained from three named human TM strains (TM62: 4+4; TM70: 2+2; TM72: 1+1) exposed to 500 nM DEX for seven days. The seven Resource samples were excluded from treatment-effect comparisons; the technical sample file count was never treated as 14 independent donor observations. A compressed processed gene-by-sample CPM matrix, not raw integer gene counts, formed the third-source transcript comparison.

### 2.1a Historical source provenance: the 2024 pre-deposition author reports

Original author-project historical sources preceding the public 2026 GEO deposit were also audited. An Australian Vision Research 2024 grant report describes 74 human TM donors, 316,799 single-cell observations and 63 increased/17 decreased glucocorticoid-associated genes. A University of Tasmania 2024 conference presentation from the same research collaboration reports 59 single-cell/genotype-QC-passing samples before genotype-based demultiplexing. The current 57 donors are the **separate present analysis's inclusion set**, determined using exact Vehicle/DEX pairing, BeamA ≥0.75 and at least 30 matched filtered-matrix cells per arm. Without an original donor-level QC crosswalk, the numerical difference between the author's 59 QC samples and our 57 paired donors cannot be attributed to particular donors or a specific exclusion mechanism. The author's identity-resolved 80 high-effect DEG list and the signed numerical threshold for the 17 downregulated genes remain unavailable; the 2024 project report and the GEO abstract describe inconsistent or mathematically ambiguous downregulated log2FC inequalities. [2024 source and audit](GSE313736_2024_ORIGINAL_SOURCE_59_VS_57_AND_AUTHOR80_HOLD_20261101.md).

### 2.2 Primary scRNA metadata inclusion, demultiplexing and cell selection

GSE313736 public 10x filtered matrix files were downloaded for 18 libraries (nine paired pool-level condition sets) with explicit gzip CRC/feature-order/coordinate-record checks. Only confident single-genotype droplet assignments mapped to valid donors were considered. Barcodes were cross-joined between classification metadata and the original 10x filtered count matrices. The primary analysis required at least **30 actually matched selected cells in both the vehicle and DEX arms** for each donor and a donor assignment to one pool. The primary BeamA classification used the deposited CellTypist label `BeamA` with a confidence threshold of at least 0.75, yielding 57 donor pairs. These labels are applied **after treatment**, a limitation intrinsic to the study.

For confidence sensitivity, the same metadata and original UMI matrices were reaggregated at BeamA confidence at least 0.50, retaining exactly the original 57 eligible donor pairs. For independent-annotation sensitivity, the scPred `TM_Fibro` annotation was used, and the analysis was restricted to the **55 donors that passed both selection definitions** with at least 30 matrix-joined cells in both conditions for each annotation. ScPred cell assignment and BeamA assignment can label some of the same cells; this sensitivity analysis does not create a new independent cohort.

The original GEO source sample-level annotation reports 48-hour treatment, but at least one individual GSM growth-protocol narrative contains a conflicting duration reference. This inconsistency should be cited in the source-provenance limitations rather than concealed; it cannot be resolved by the transcript data alone.

### 2.3 Raw integer count pseudobulk aggregation and differential-expression models

Within each selected donor-condition profile, matrix-market `gene × cell` integer UMI counts were summed into a donor-level raw-count vector. Paired vehicle and DEX samples were kept distinct, and source-pool assignments were checked. Biological replication for the single-cell discovery study was thus counted at the donor level, **not at the cell level**.

Gene expression modeling used edgeR negative-binomial quasi-likelihood procedures with `model.matrix(~ pair_id + treatment)`, TMM library normalization and robust dispersion/QL fitting (`estimateDisp(..., robust=TRUE)` and `glmQLFit(..., robust=TRUE)`). Expression filtering used `filterByExpr` with treatment grouping and original library-size information, and treatment-associated expression estimates were evaluated by `glmQLFTest`. Gene-level multiple testing used Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (BH-FDR). The original 0.75-confidence analysis tested 16,050 genes, while 0.50-confidence reanalysis tested 16,728; differences in the testable universe were retained explicitly instead of treating filtered genes as zero or nonsignificant.

The fixed 55-donor dual-annotation comparison used the **same** paired edgeR design for BeamA and TM_Fibro, with 16,051 and 14,003 genes respectively passing expression filters. Gene-wise concordance calculations were restricted to common Ensembl IDs testable in both models, and any selected candidate filtered from one model was marked untestable rather than assigned a pseudo-statistic. No clinical steroid-challenge phenotype was present.

### 2.3a Original-library identifiability and pool-composition QC

To evaluate whether treatment-associated RNA patterns could be distinguished from original processing-library nuisance effects, we reconstructed a *design-matrix audit*, rather than refitting a spurious causal batch-adjusted edgeR model. The original nine source donor pools were each processed into one Vehicle and one DEX 10x library, yielding 18 pool-arm profiles. We computed matrix ranks for (i) nine source-pool indicator columns plus a DEX-arm column and (ii) 18 unrestricted original-library indicator columns plus that DEX column. The latter evaluates **structural identifiability**, not the size of any unobserved batch effect. We also re-read the previously archived P5e aggregate cell classification percentages and original per-pool singlet counts and decomposed the weighted pooled DEX−Vehicle BeamA≥0.75 fraction difference into *symmetric within-pool fraction changes* and *changes in arm-specific pool weights* (Kitagawa arithmetic identity). We compared these aggregate estimates with the median of 59 donor-paired selection fractions, the original nine-pool edgeR sensitivity and the anonymous MDS R² summary. No raw 10x matrices, donor count profiles or individual records were downloaded or exported in the new audit.

### 2.4 External gene identity mapping, sign consistency and third-study estimand

Genes were mapped by matched Ensembl IDs, with version stripping where documented. For the published 100 nM, 48-hour comparison, we used published gene-level log2 fold-change and adjusted significance values. For the 500 nM, seven-day study, treatment replicates were first averaged **within** each TM strain and condition; a separate descriptive `log2((mean CPM_DEX+1)/(mean CPM_vehicle+1))` was calculated for each of the three strains, and the three strain-specific values were equally averaged. The pseudocount was **one CPM**, and the three strains—not the seven files per arm—were the observational unit for descriptive direction assessment.

We did **not** apply edgeR or DESeq2 to the third study's processed CPM as if it were raw counts, did **not** report a third-study gene-wise FDR or claim a 48-hour versus seven-day causal time contrast, and did **not** pool its pseudocount log-ratio with the 48-hour published model-based log2FC into a numerical effect-size meta-analysis. Directional agreement was reported only for genes with mapped, finite values in the relevant source.

### 2.4a Post-erratum public raw RNA-seq source-access audit (provenance only)

Following the October 2026 data-availability addendum, NCBI SRA accession PRJNA1243143 was independently inventoried in the official ENA read-run metadata. Forty-six paired-end runs (92 FASTQ files, 89.68 GB compressed) were deposited across 23 nominal DEX/vehicle library pairs with TM/SCE source labels, and the previously ambiguously named pairs were resolved through official BioSample XML treatment fields. However, none of the 23 representative BioSamples provided an explicit exposure duration, so the public run records themselves did not establish a source-verified 1h/6h/48h assignment.

The complete four-file publication supplement was separately retrieved from the official Europe PMC archive with checksum verification. Its source workbook includes 63 timepoint-specific differential-expression/pathway and marker-expression sheets. Source Table 82 names nine human TM strains for the 2-day timepoint, four for 6 hours and three for 1 hour, while Table 83 provides the corresponding SCE source strain groups. Cross-reference of these strain sets with the deposited library alias naming conventions yields nine **source-consistent candidate** 2-day TM treatment-control pairs (18 runs, 36 FASTQ files, 32.71 GB compressed). The candidate matches are **not a direct author-confirmed run-to-timepoint crosswalk**, since the 46-run archive records lack exposure-time fields. A frozen-source, three-way comparison of the article's original Table 1, the independently archived Supplementary Table 82/83 strain-by-timepoint marker columns, and public SRA library labels found agreement for all nine 2-day TM strains but **one discrepancy in the 6-hour TM group**: Table 1 lists TM155 at 6 hours, while Table 82's 6-hour TM expression columns and the corresponding SRA alias candidate instead list TM151. This conflict was retained in the source-provenance audit rather than resolved by assuming which publication table is correct; no sample-specific SRA exposure-hour metadata were available. These repeated references are sections of one publication, not independent biological experiments. [Table 1/S003/SRA discrepancy audit](MEHROTRA_TABLE1_S003_SRA_TRIPLE_SOURCE_20261101.md). No complete per-sample raw integer featureCounts matrix was confirmed in the publication's four supplements; the published full-gene DE result tables and selected marker TPM values were retained without being mistaken for original raw counts. Consequently, raw FASTQ realignment and independent model re-fitting were **not performed** in this investigation. [Full source provenance and gate](MEHROTRA_SRA_23PAIR_TIMEPOINT_AND_RAW_COUNTS_GATE_20261101.md).

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Reconstructed primary donor-unit DEX-associated effects

In the 57 donor-paired BeamA ≥0.75 analysis, 16,050 genes passed filtering, of which **13,717 (85.5%)** met BH-FDR <0.05. Such an unusually large fraction must be evaluated cautiously because the original treatment and vehicle material were processed in different 10x libraries, and library-level artifacts and post-exposure annotation selection may align with condition. Thus FDR measures uncertainty conditional on the fitted model and does not isolate an unconfounded pharmacological effect.

### 3.2 Cross-study transcript directions, with exposure-duration and estimator caveats (Figure 1)

MYOC exhibited positive signed changes and TGFBI and MMP2 negative signed changes in the primary 500 nM, 48-hour BeamA analysis, the published 100 nM, 48-hour bulk study, and the 500 nM, seven-day TM study. In the respective source effect metrics, MYOC estimates were **+4.026, +1.860, +4.349**, TGFBI **−1.869, −0.843, −1.529** and MMP2 **−0.589, −0.357, −0.756**. The third-study figures are strain-averaged, CPM-based pseudocount log-ratios and therefore cannot be interpreted as comparable model-based effect magnitudes. ACTA2 was positive in both 48-hour sources (**+0.469, +0.374**) but negative in the seven-day source (**−0.399**), indicating experimental-context-dependent directional discordance rather than evidence of a causal time reversal.

The official October 2026 erratum does not state any correction to numerical gene-level values; it adds PRJNA1243143 to the Data Availability section. The published summary estimates were therefore not modified on account of the notice. Raw SRA sample-level inference remains pending a validated timepoint/treatment/strain mapping. No equivalent third-study gene-wise inferential statistics were calculated.

### 3.3 Within-source confidence-threshold sensitivity (Figure 2A)

Reaggregation at BeamA confidence ≥0.50 preserved the original 57 paired donor identities but increased the number of matrix-joined selected cells. The original and relaxed filter shared 16,050 testable Ensembl genes and yielded a log2FC Pearson correlation of **0.99911597** and median shared absolute difference of **0.0112462**. MYOC, TGFBI and MMP2 retained effect directions and BH-FDR<0.05. An internally defined large-effect gene screen (`abs(log2FC)≥3`, BH-FDR<0.05) gave 83 versus 88 genes, of which 81 overlapped; the remainder were largely attributable to magnitude thresholds or changed expression-test universes. This **does not constitute an identity-level comparison with the original GEO authors' reported 80 high-effect genes**, whose individual locus list is not yet verified.

### 3.4 Same-donor alternative cell-annotation sensitivity (Figure 2B)

Under the restricted 55 donors eligible in both annotation schemes, BeamA and scPred TM_Fibro yielded 16,051 and 14,003 testable genes; **13,975** Ensembl loci were common. The log2FC Pearson correlation for common genes was **0.94990493**, **12,634/13,975** effect directions agreed, and **10,255** were BH-FDR significant in both models. All three leading candidates retained their direction and statistical significance: MYOC **+4.006/+3.525**, TGFBI **−1.868/−1.941** and MMP2 **−0.591/−0.459** (BeamA/scPred). Of 16 legacy preselected genes, only 15 were testable in both; all 15 were sign concordant and FDR significant, whereas **ERFE was filtered from the TM_Fibro model** and was not assigned a synthetic log2FC/FDR estimate.

Because the alternative annotations reuse donors, libraries and potentially some individual cells, their p-values cannot be regarded as independent replication tests. The high number of jointly significant genes likewise does not eliminate correlated technical biases.

### 3.4a Exploratory effect-magnitude and abundance-stratified annotation robustness (Supplementary Figure)

To assess whether the high overall within-source model correlation concealed instability for weak or low-abundance genes, we reprocessed **only already fitted public aggregate gene-level edgeR statistics**, without repeating 10x raw-matrix downloads. Tested Ensembl genes shared between each pair of models were stratified by *baseline* absolute log2FC (less than 0.25, 0.25–1, 1–3 and 3 or greater) and baseline logCPM quartile. We computed sign agreement, the number of genes meeting BH-FDR<0.05 in both models and median absolute effect difference. These strata were exploratory and source-dependent, not independent validation sets.

For the matched **55-donor BeamA/scPred** comparison, among genes with baseline abs(log2FC)<0.25, sign concordance was **78.26% (n=5,349)**, compared with **97.67% (n=7,583)** at 0.25–1, **99.90% (n=984)** at 1–3 and **100% (n=59)** at ≥3. Sign concordance across baseline expression quartiles increased from **87.06% in Q1** to **93.39% in Q4**. For the same-57-donor BeamA threshold comparison, sign concordance was **96.66% for baseline abs(log2FC)<0.25** and 100% in the three larger-effect strata. The matched-55 gene-level median absolute log2FC difference was **0.10190**, compared with **0.01125** under the 57-donor confidence threshold adjustment.

These descriptive results limit general claims based solely on aggregate Pearson correlation and support prioritizing candidates that have sufficiently large, interpretable effects rather than significant but small changes. High agreement among genes *selected by large baseline effects* is not independent replication and cannot correct condition-confounded 10x library preparation. Source logs, SHA-256 manifests and exact subgroup denominators are preserved in [real CI #41389639386](https://github.com/kimtk94/IS_Analysis_V3/actions/runs/41389639386), [figure and table archive #13116138830](https://github.com/kimtk94/IS_Analysis_V3/actions/runs/41389639386/artifacts/13116138830), and [frozen stratum report](GATE_B_STRATIFIED_EFFECT_REPRODUCIBILITY_REAL_20261102.md).

### 3.4b Structural treatment–library aliasing and pooled-cell selection decomposition (Supplementary Methods)

The original **nine pool × two treatment-arm** profiles supported a design with nine pool indicators and one DEX indicator (rank **10/10**, residual df 8). Once the **18 separately prepared original 10x library effects** were freely parameterized, the DEX column became the sum of the nine DEX-library indicator vectors: the combined **19-column design had rank 18**, with **zero rank increment** attributable to the treatment column. Thus the design cannot separately estimate pharmacological DEX and unrestricted original-library nuisance effects. This does not demonstrate that the DEX response is an artifact; it sets a formal **non-identifiability boundary** for causal attribution based on these data alone.

The original pooled BeamA≥0.75 cell fraction was approximately **2.4064 percentage points** higher in DEX than Vehicle. Using all nine source pools' original singlet-count weights and six-decimal published annotation proportions, a symmetric pool-weight decomposition gave **+0.868638 pp from arm differences within pools** and **+1.537701 pp from changes in the arm-dependent pool weights**, summing to the observed **+2.406339 pp** to rounding precision. The weight term represented **63.9% of the descriptive aggregate contrast**, *not* an estimated percentage of drug response explained by technical batch, capture or classifier bias. In contrast, the median donor-paired fraction change across **59 paired donors** was **−0.421 pp**, with 29 positive and 30 negative signs; no directional median shift was supported by the two-sided sign test (p=1.000). Pooled cell-count and donor-median estimands therefore differ materially.

Existing statistical sensitivity remained numerically high: among **16,049** genes tested in both the original 57-donor model and conservative nine-pool-library model, **11,079/13,716** originally FDR-significant genes also passed the pooled-library model; the gene-level log2FC correlation was **0.99624485**. The original unadjusted MDS-axis-1 treatment-only R² was **0.95557721**, but this value cannot be labeled the proportion of variance causally explained by DEX. Neither model robustness nor visual separation can overcome the treatment/library rank aliasing. Source-code tests and real aggregate verification are at [CI #41430965326](https://github.com/kimtk94/IS_Analysis_V3/actions/runs/41430965326), [aggregate report #13133928461](https://github.com/kimtk94/IS_Analysis_V3/actions/runs/41430965326/artifacts/13133928461), and [formal design audit](GSE313736_LIBRARY_TREATMENT_ALIASING_AND_POOL_WEIGHT_DECOMPOSITION_20261102.md).

### 3.5 Exploratory EMT direction stability across three experiments (Figure 3)

Among 194 jointly mapped Hallmark EMT genes, **141** effect directions agreed between the two 48-hour sources, **156** between 500 nM 48-hour and 500 nM seven-day sources, and **145** between 100 nM 48-hour and 500 nM seven-day sources. Across all three settings, **124/194 (63.9%)** had the same direction. The set was selected in follow-up analysis and the constituent genes are correlated; these results are descriptive and do not establish pathway-level independent replication or actual TM fibrosis.

### 3.5a Individual 7-day TM strain contributions and leave-one-strain-out sensitivity (Supplementary Table)

The prior three-study comparison used an **equal-strain mean** of the processed seven-day GSE301517 CPM log-ratios. To establish whether its signed directions depended on a single strain, we re-audited the archived published gene-level **TM62, TM70 and TM72** log2 CPM+1 ratio components and recalculated the mean while excluding one strain at a time. The original source contains **four, two and one technical measurements per arm** for these strains, respectively; these measurements were averaged within strain before strain-level summary and cannot be counted as seven biological donors. The three strain groups' independent donor provenance and 7-day gene-wise inferential FDR remain unverified.

**MYOC** increased in each 7-day strain (TM62 **+4.822**, TM70 **+3.648**, TM72 **+4.576**), whereas **TGFBI** decreased (**−2.300**, **−1.344**, **−0.945**) and **MMP2** decreased (**−0.835**, **−1.271**, **−0.162**). For all three genes, excluding any one strain preserved the same direction as the 500nM 48-hour GSE313736 and published 100nM 48-hour Mehrotra results. The weak magnitude of MMP2 in TM72 emphasizes that this is a **direction-only** consistency finding. By contrast, ACTA2 was near zero in TM62 (**+0.005**) and negative in TM70 (**−0.760**) and TM72 (**−0.442**), consistent with its failure to show a universal cross-experiment sign; this does **not** establish a controlled time-dependent reversal.

Among the 16 earlier selected P9 candidates, the 7-day equal-strain mean had the same sign as the original 48-hour source for **10/16**, all three individual 7-day strains matched for **7/16**, and all three leave-one-strain-out equal means matched for **8/16**. These distinct criteria cannot be interchanged. Of 194 mapped Hallmark EMT genes, **124/194** had consistent signed *aggregate* effects in all three studies, but only **90/194 (46.4%)** additionally had matching directions in **all three separate GSE301517 strains**. The 7-day strain-to-acute sign-support distribution was **12, 25, 54, 103** genes with 0, 1, 2, 3 concordant strains, respectively.

These sensitivity analyses are exploratory, use correlated genes, and do not provide strain-level independent p-values, a standardized three-study effect-size meta-analysis or identification of a causal DEX/time/batch mechanism. The empirical source files, per-gene strata and SHA-256 are recorded by [GitHub Actions #41423487666](https://github.com/kimtk94/IS_Analysis_V3/actions/runs/41423487666), [published aggregate result #13130831706](https://github.com/kimtk94/IS_Analysis_V3/actions/runs/41423487666/artifacts/13130831706), and the [supplementary methods/results report](GSE301517_7DAY_STRAIN_LOO_AND_EMT_STRICT_REPLICATION_20261102.md).

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Central interpretation and contribution

The data support a narrow but reproducible claim: **glucocorticoid-associated MYOC induction and TGFBI/MMP2 reduction at the transcript level were directionally consistent across three experimental sources** and were robust to two tested cell-classification strategies within the large single-cell source. The principal contribution is not a novel DEX target or previously unknown EMT pathway; rather, it is a transparent cross-study and annotation-sensitivity audit that handles donor-level pseudoreplication, effects of selection thresholds, discrepancies in source expression estimators and incomplete cross-paper provenance.

### 4.2 Why context-discordant genes matter

ACTA2 increased in the two 48-hour sources but showed a negative average ratio in the seven-day third study. Several other candidate genes likewise exhibited different signed responses by source. These differences are not interpretable as direct evidence of temporal or dose-dependent reversal because the studies differ in cell strains, culture preparation, experimental conditions, treatment duration, library methods and statistical estimands. The negative evidence argues against promoting every strongly significant DEG in one study to a generalizable GC-response marker.

### 4.3 Main limitation: DEX and 10x library preparation are not disentangled

Even robust pairing of cell-derived expression profiles by donor is not the same as fully randomized multiplexed experimental preparation. The original 18 10x libraries were assigned by condition, and treatment and processing/libraries are aligned; the high fraction of FDR significant genes raises concern that some apparent magnitude may be technical or related to exposure-driven cell selection. The two cell-classification reruns change annotation but retain the same underlying libraries and donors. Accordingly, the observed numerical stability of MYOC/TGFBI/MMP2 is **not sufficient proof of their causal response in an independent, batch-balanced same-donor experiment**.

### 4.3a Exact experimental-design identifiability ceiling

Separate library preparation by drug arm introduces a key distinction between *estimating a modeled DEX-associated contrast* and *identifying a pharmacologic drug effect free of arbitrary library nuisance*. Because the treatment indicator is exactly spanned by source-library indicators, an unconstrained library-effect correction cannot be fit as if DEX remained independently estimable. The median paired-donor BeamA fraction and pooled-cell fraction also give different answers: symmetric pool weight redistribution accounts arithmetically for 1.538 of 2.406 pooled percentage points, yet **cannot be interpreted as a causal mediation or batch-attribution fraction**. The formal non-identifiability and capture-weight findings narrow the interpretation of the originally very high DEG rate and prioritize independent batch-balanced TM experiments for definitive mechanism inference. They do not invalidate all within-culture transcript observations, and the three candidate transcripts' external directional support is reported separately.

### 4.4 Pathway annotation and functional endpoint boundaries

The EMT label refers to a curated gene set and should not automatically be interpreted as a histological epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition in already mesenchymal-like TM cells. A decrease in MMP2 RNA is compatible with hypotheses about reduced matrix remodeling but says nothing definitive about extracellular metalloproteinase abundance, enzymatic activity or outflow facility. TGFBI and MYOC also require orthogonal protein evidence and, for physiological mechanism, targeted functional assays. Earlier publications have already established substantial DEX-related matrix, oxidative-stress and senescence biology, so transcript associations alone cannot be marketed as an entirely new mechanism.

### 4.4a Original sample metadata disagreement between publication sources

A focused source audit further revealed a within-publication discrepancy: the original article's Table 1 assigns TM155 to its six-hour TM experiment, while the official supplementary expression table includes TM151 rather than TM155 among its six-hour TM strain labels. The mapped SRA labels are consistent with the latter naming scheme but are not independently time-stamped by an original BioSample treatment-duration attribute. The nine reported 48-hour TM strain names agree between the published table and supplement, supporting a *candidate* 48-hour source subset, but not an author-confirmed run/timepoint/QC linkage. Consequently, an RNA-seq re-fit of the newly released raw source was not attempted. Resolving the six-hour discrepancy and obtaining the original treatment-time sample sheet would be necessary before any across-time analysis.

### 4.4b Origin of the published six-hour TM strain-source mismatch

Official 2026 bioRxiv preprint and final peer-reviewed IOVS article versions were compared using their original XML Table 1 after normalizing written exposure-duration units such as `hours` and `h`. Both versions consistently list **TM151 for one hour and two days** and **TM155 for six hours and two days**. The published Supplementary Table 82 contains a six-hour TM151 expression column, not a six-hour TM155 column, indicating the source disagreement was **already present in the preprint-era demographic table** and has not been resolved by the final article or its October Data Availability erratum. We did not assume either publication component was correct and did not interpret the resulting SRA six-hour candidate as author-verified. The nine two-day TM strain names agree across both source manuscript versions and official supplement, but source-specific SRA RUN duration and post-QC inclusion remain unconfirmed. [Publisher-version audit](MEHROTRA_PREPRINT_JOURNAL_S003_CONFLICT_ORIGIN_20261101.md).

### 4.5 Reproducibility and publication limitations

The source GSE313736 GEO summary reports 63 increases and 17 decreases beyond a stated high-effect threshold, but its deposited Series materials do not expose the complete original authors' list of gene identities. The present project's internal 83 versus 88 high-effect gene analysis therefore does not verify the original 80. In addition, the external journal article's October 2026 erratum has now been checked: it adds an SRA Data Availability statement without reporting gene-effect corrections. An authenticated 46-run source archive is accessible, but raw FASTQ count-level confirmation remains unperformed. The third independent public study currently available as a preprint used normalized CPM values and three primary TM strains, so the present cross-paper comparison is a descriptive sign check, not a third independent gene-wise statistical test. No linked clinical steroid-induced ∆IOP outcome was available for prediction.

### 4.6 Outlook

The logical wet-lab next experiment would use independent human TM cultures from the **same donors** split into randomized vehicle, 100 nM and 500 nM DEX arms, measured with a common 48-hour time point and independently randomized extraction and assay batches. A 1-hour/6-hour/48-hour factorial could be added after an appropriate pilot sample-size calculation. Pre-specified RT-qPCR of MYOC/TGFBI/MMP2, secreted MYOC measurement, MMP2 protein/zymography and ECM functional phenotyping could test whether stable transcript-associated candidates are relevant to matrix mechanics and aqueous humor outflow. Clinical prediction would require a separate phenotyped steroid-response cohort with actual ∆IOP observations.

## 5. Conclusion — permitted final wording

A donor-level reanalysis of public TM single-cell RNA-seq identified MYOC, TGFBI and MMP2 as transcript-level candidates with concordant effect directions in two other public TM studies. The observed signed association persisted across the tested confidence and cell-annotation sensitivity analyses, while ACTA2 and other genes illustrated the limits of contextual generalization. These results constitute **computational prioritization and robustness evidence**, not causal, proteomic or patient-level glaucoma-risk validation.
