# Ischemic stroke manuscript draft — 2026-11-25

## Title
Cross-ancestry and subtype-specific contrasts of reported genetic effects in ischemic stroke: a retrospective fixed-marker analysis of GIGASTROKE

**Status:** Working thesis/manuscript draft, not submitted. Original discovery/replication independence, model-scale comparability and author/ethics review remain unresolved. The analysis is not a new GWAS, a prospective hypothesis test, or a formal MR/colocalization analysis.

## Abstract
**Background.** Ischemic stroke encompasses clinically distinct subtypes, and the interpretation of reported variant effects across ancestries is complicated by phenotype ascertainment, cohort composition, linkage disequilibrium and incomplete variant coverage.

**Methods.** We retrospectively froze a panel of 24 historically East Asian (EAS)-selected regional representatives and evaluated reported effect estimates for ischemic stroke (AIS), cardioembolic (CES), large-artery (LAS) and small-vessel stroke (SVS) in EAS and European (EUR) GIGASTROKE summaries. Exact coordinate, allele-pair and rsID matching and historical EAS AIS estimate parity were required. Eight contrasts per marker were retained, including unresolved cells, and Bonferroni correction was applied across 192 comparisons. A maximum-variance covariance bound served as a conservative sensitivity analysis. Regional association patterns and public molecular QTL evidence were examined descriptively for two post-readout candidates.

**Results.** 170/192 marker-study cells and 158/192 contrasts were evaluable. Eleven contrasts passed the family correction under zero covariance; four passed under the maximum-variance bound, comprising two known PITX2-region subtype contrasts, rs2767060 for EAS versus EUR LAS (difference 0.3672; adjusted P=.03283), and rs7203740 for EAS SVS versus CES (difference -0.2945; adjusted P=.04376). In the fixed rs2767060 region, the index ranked first for EAS LAS association and the ancestry contrast among 1,099 matched variants, but no matched variant reached conventional genome-wide significance. The rs7203740 C/T allele was nominally associated with PLCG2 expression in ROSMAP DLPFC; the eQTL lead differed and regional association-strength correspondence was weak. Neither candidate has independent subtype replication or established molecular causality.

**Conclusions.** Fixed-marker contrasts can describe differences in reported association patterns and expose limitations in cross-ancestry and subtype comparisons. These retrospective, conditional results motivate targeted independent validation, not claims of novel loci, ancestry-specific causal effects or therapeutic targets.

## Introduction
The GIGASTROKE consortium substantially expanded cross-ancestry stroke genetic discovery and incorporated subtype GWAS, functional annotation and risk prediction [1]. Recent studies have already integrated stroke subtype GWAS with brain and blood cell-specific eQTL data [2], or combined cell-specific and bulk molecular QTL with SMR, MR and colocalization [3]. A general integration of stroke GWAS and eQTL data is therefore not a new research premise. In particular, [2] reported cell-specific CES/SVS candidate genes but no LAS finding under its own design, while [3] reported LAS candidates in its distinct analysis. Neither observation establishes novelty of our candidate marker.

We focus on a narrower question: what patterns of differences in reported beta estimates can be observed across the two ancestry labels and four phenotypes for an unchanged historically selected marker set, under explicit alignment, missingness, multiplicity and conservative covariance treatment? The aim is to prioritize candidate comparisons for independent validation while retaining both supportive and unfavorable evidence. Ancestry labels refer to source study categories; they are not discrete biological or national identities.

### Fixed contrast family
For each of 24 markers compare EAS minus EUR in AIS, CES, LAS and SVS; within each ancestry compare SVS minus CES and SVS minus LAS. This yields 192 fixed extension contrasts, independent of missingness. Let d = beta1 - beta2 and s1,s2 be reported standard errors. Conditional on comparable effect scales and approximately normal errors, zero covariance gives SE(d)=sqrt(s1^2+s2^2). By the covariance inequality, Var(d) is bounded above by (s1+s2)^2 when the marginal variances are valid. Use s1+s2 for the maximum-variance screen, a two-sided normal-tail P and individual 95% interval d ± 1.959964(s1+s2). The bound is not an estimated covariance, an exact interaction test, or a remedy for ascertainment and scale differences. Apply Bonferroni P_adj=min(1,192P) separately to the two variance assumptions within this retrospective extension. These are not project-wide error rates; selection of markers using historical EAS associations means even corrected results are exploratory. The interval is not simultaneous or multiplicity-adjusted. Missing contrasts remain HOLD, not zero or null associations.

### Post-readout regional and molecular follow-up
The known PITX2 region is a contextual positive control. The two non-PITX2 markers passing the conservative fixed-family screen were carried forward without declaring additional significance families. For rs2767060, compare reference high-LD proxy membership (Ensembl 1000 Genomes Phase 3 CHB, JPT, CEU; r^2 >= .8, within 500 kb of the anchor) and a fixed 1 Mb GRCh38 window using exact nonpalindromic matched variant keys. These reference LD relationships are not cohort-specific LD matrices or certified phase. Rank observed association strengths and conditional max-variance contrasts; nominal proxy counts are descriptive, not independent replications. Compare the exact index/proxy tokens against the pinned GIGASTROKE supplementary workbook and the complete bounded GWAS Catalog regional variant lists from release 2026-11-17; use exact-index association lookups and rs2129981 search control. Catalog scope, date and matching rule must be stated, with no comprehensive novelty assertion.

For rs7203740, preserve exact GRCh38 C/T identity and candidate context across five eQTL Catalogue sources: BLUEPRINT monocytes, GTEx blood, aorta, cortex, ROSMAP DLPFC. Nominal associations are exploratory; neither tissue specificity nor causal regulation follows from a smaller P in one tissue. Use official permutation p_beta and BH over 23,076 tested genes for the PLCG2 gene-level result, separately from nominal SNP estimates. The lead identity agrees but effect estimates differ between publisher files; cross-file equivalence is unresolved. Full official ROSMAP SuSiE CS read and exact versioned gene-ID check establish absence from that file, not PIP=0. For the fixed chr16 1 Mb window, evaluate nominal-only exact-coordinate/allele/rsID matches to EAS and EUR CES/SVS, report pairwise coverage and their common intersection. Use descriptive Spearman ranks and top-set overlap with ties; LD dependence precludes treating individual SNPs as independent observations or correlations as formal colocalization. No formal posterior, MR, mediation or causal inference is performed.

### Independent validation design
No external estimates have been obtained. A draft 24-marker request across four phenotypes (96 rows), exact-identity response QC and pre-readout eligibility protocol are available. Primary external endpoint must be chosen before obtaining effects using an authorized cohort with non-overlap, comparable phenotype, stroke-free controls, model, quality and sample size. Do not treat case-only subtype differences as case-control replication. Multiple jointly claimed phenotype families require further multiplicity handling. Illustrative LAS power scenarios are planning only, not cohort power. An EAS LAS replication alone cannot establish a replicated EAS–EUR contrast.

## Results
Of 192 marker-study cells from 24 historically selected EAS regional markers and eight GIGASTROKE ancestry/phenotype summaries, 170 were matched and 158/192 fixed contrasts were evaluable. Eleven contrasts passed Bonferroni correction across 192 under a zero-covariance assumption and four passed a maximum-variance bound. The latter included the known PITX2-region marker rs2129981 in EUR and EAS SVS-versus-CES contrasts (delta -0.2983 and -0.3118; adjusted P 3.49e-6 and 8.43e-4), rs2767060 in EAS-versus-EUR LAS (delta +0.3672, adjusted P 0.03283), and rs7203740 in EAS SVS-versus-CES (delta -0.2945, adjusted P 0.04376). These are conditional comparisons of reported coefficients from the same release, not direct case-case or causal estimates. Retrospective EAS marker selection, phenotype ascertainment, population LD and sample imbalance, model-scale comparability and independent replication remain unresolved.

At rs2767060, the exact C/T T-allele association in EAS LAS (beta 0.3582, SE 0.0680, P 1.38e-7) did not meet conventional genome-wide significance, while the EUR LAS association was near zero (beta -0.0090, SE 0.0297, P 0.7604). The 1000 Genomes Phase 3 reference comparison returned 78 unique high-LD proxies across CHB/JPT/CEU, 15 shared. No exact index or proxy token occurred in the pinned GIGASTROKE supplementary workbook or in the 51 Catalog regional variants retrieved from the 2026-11-17 release. The variant remains intergenic; no causal gene is established. Regional and independent literature coverage remains incomplete.

At rs7203740, the exact C/T (not C/A) T allele showed nominal PLCG2 association in ROSMAP DLPFC (beta 0.279006, SE 0.068636, P 5.51034e-5); the other four tested datasets did not have nominal P < 0.05 for PLCG2. The gene-level permutation BH P was 4.5327492e-6 among 23,076 tested genes. Its official lead rs11640696 T/G at chr16:81878856 differs from the GWAS candidate. Nominal and permutation files agree on lead variant identity and direction but not the exact estimates (nominal beta -0.249511 and P 1.04157e-10; permutation beta -0.246995 and nominal P 7.86674e-11). The mismatch remains a model/source-equivalence HOLD. Full hash-verified ROSMAP SuSiE output (377,180 rows, 7,083 gene IDs) contained no PLCG2 gene row or rs7203740 variant; no PIP can be assigned. The Catalog region contained 342 curated variants but no exact rs7203740 match. This is exploratory molecular context, not evidence of tissue specificity, colocalization, or mediation.

### Regional association coverage and figure readback

A separate nominal-only descriptive regional analysis preserved the failed permutation/nominal equivalence gate and reproduced both nominal anchor rows. In the fixed chr16:81,275,211–82,275,211 interval, 5,771 PLCG2 rows collapsed to 5,363 unique valid variants (408 identical aliases); 3,555 strict nonpalindromic SNPs were eligible. Of these, EAS CES matched 2,230 (62.7%), EAS SVS 2,398 (67.5%), EUR CES 2,963 (83.3%), and EUR SVS 2,612 (73.5%). The four-study intersection contained 1,966 variants (55.3%). rs7203740 C/T matched the two EAS subtypes only; rs11640696 T/G matched all four. The strongest matched EAS CES signal was rs7203740 (P 9.198e-7) whereas the strongest nominal PLCG2 eQTL was rs11640696 (P 1.04157e-10; EAS CES P 0.2334). None of the matched variants in any of the four comparisons had GWAS P < 5e-8. This does not exclude shared causal signal; power, incomplete coverage, distinct ancestry/LD and multiple signals may contribute. The shared 1,966-variant subset excludes the original candidate, so it cannot alone assess that variant. The nominal source has AN=1120 across observed unique variants, not certified regression N or sdY. The fixed interval captures only part of the reported 11,248 permutation-tested cis variants; counts from different files must not be treated as identical testing universes. Formal colocalization remains HOLD.

### Descriptive association profiles and candidate decision

Pairwise-matched Spearman correlations of -log10(P) between ROSMAP PLCG2 nominal QTL and EAS CES, EAS SVS, EUR CES, EUR SVS were 0.1072, -0.0394, -0.0331, 0.0445; in the fixed four-study intersection they were 0.1048, -0.0812, -0.0121, 0.0308. No overlap among the top ten variants in any comparison (both sets); pairwise top-50 intersections 2,0,0,2. EAS CES index rs7203740 was ranked first in GWAS and 58th in QTL; eQTL lead rs11640696 was ranked 503–505 in EAS CES. Signed Z rank correlations, unlike association-strength correlations, were 0.0431, 0.0557, 0.3003 and 0.3495 pairwise and 0.0620, 0.0962, 0.4266 and 0.2906 in the intersection. LD-correlated SNPs are not independent observations. None of these summaries supplies a test of shared causal variants, a formal negative colocalization, or tissue-specific causation. The common set excludes the index SNP and ancestry-matched molecular data are lacking.

Decision: retain PLCG2 as an exploratory molecular context candidate with weak regional support for the proposed EAS CES mechanism; do not promote it to the primary causal claim. Prioritize independently specified region-level LAS heterogeneity around rs2767060 and external subtype replication; do not introduce additional positive-candidate selection. The completed profiles and two supplementary figures are in [run 45561026204](https://github.com/kimtk94/IS_Analysis_V3/actions/runs/45561026204/artifacts/14997881907). Actual stdout is archived in `evidence/is_plcg2_association_profiles_20261125.json`; technical figure generation only, no visual QA.

## Discussion
The known PITX2-region subtype contrasts provide a clinically plausible check on the reported-effect alignment, not new discovery or independent replication. The rs2767060 association is the higher-priority exploratory candidate: a coherent correlated regional pattern with contrasting LAS effects, below conventional genome-wide significance, and a large allele-frequency difference. Its observed effect may reflect sampling, selection, LD, phenotype classification, study composition or true heterogeneity. The present data cannot distinguish these explanations. Korean stroke registry data and GENESIS-K may help frame an external study, but a registry is not automatically a valid case-control GWAS. A BBJ-derived result may overlap discovery. No claim that absence from a curated association database demonstrates a new locus.

For PLCG2, gene-level eQTL evidence is distinct from evidence that the stroke association is mediated by expression. Regional peak discordance, weak association-strength rank correlations, unequal variant coverage, absence of published credible-set entries and nominal/permutation estimate differences leave the proposed shared-signal mechanism unresolved. This is an important unfavorable finding, not a formal negative colocalization. It is not valid to assume tissue specificity based on differences in nominal P across unequal cohorts. Molecular ancestry and regression sample size/sdY are not certified, and appropriate LD matrices and full cis coverage are not established. We retain PLCG2 only as exploratory context.

The main limitations are retrospective EAS selection, same-release reuse, unknown overlap and covariance, partial marker and regional coverage, unverified full-file identity and model comparability, cohort imbalance, non-independent correlated proxy variants, and limited sample size for subtype outcomes. A conservative bound addresses only unknown covariance under the stated model assumptions. It cannot remove selection or phenotype bias. Prior subtype-specific QTL studies [2,3] already address biological questions beyond our descriptive methods; therefore our contribution is transparency of this fixed-marker comparison and explicit evidence/eligibility gates, with the biological significance requiring independent confirmation. Publication suitability is conditional. The next scientific milestone is external subtype data, not repeated positive-candidate searches.
